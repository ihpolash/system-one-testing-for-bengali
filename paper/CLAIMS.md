# Claim set (written before drafting; every number in the paper comes from analysis/tables or analysis/*.json through paper/numbers.tex)

| # | Claim (scoped) | Evidence | Strongest objection |
|---|---|---|---|
| C1 | Rule-based romanization costs models more accuracy than AI-generated Banglish. On 877 whole-text items, rule-based romanization costs Jev 19.4 points and the author's Banglish 11.2; the Banglish is easier than the rule-based text by 8.2 points (95% CI 5.1 to 11.1, seed of make_tables), by 13.6 on MASSIVE, 8.6 on XNLI-bn pairs, 0.0 on SentNoB. | Table 3, Fig. 1, Fig. 2 (analysis/human_models.py) | One typist; MASSIVE is 56% of the items; XNLI-bn only 58 pairs; datasets are translated |
| C2 | The overstatement is not a ranking problem: model order under rule-based romanization matches the order under AI-generated Banglish (Kendall tau 0.89 rule-based and 0.94 natural-style against hand-written, over eight models (generated by make_tables.py; earlier hand-quoted 0.93/1.00 used a different model set)). | Table 3, validation doc sec. 11 | Eight models on the same items are not independent; ties at the bottom |
| C3 | Cross-model standing on Bengali script: Jev has the highest point estimate on all five tasks among full-set models (intervals overlap with d3-nano on XNLI-bn); d3-flash (4-bit, 400-item subset) is level with or above Jev on three; a generic 4B-effective LLM scored by option probabilities (Gemma-4-E4B) is level with d3-mini and the most robust open model to romanization; the Bengali-specific laya-bangla improves its base model on trained tasks but stays far below. | Table 2, Fig. 3 | Different prompts/pipelines; d3-flash quantised and subsampled; possible contamination |
| C4 | Open decision models are overconfident on Bengali, and one temperature fitted on a disjoint dev split removes most of it (e.g. XNLI-bn ECE for Julia 0.42 to 0.10); the correction does not transfer fully to romanized text. | Table 4, Fig. 4 | Exploratory; gate results rest on few accepted items |
| C5 | Option-order sensitivity concentrates in encoder models (answers identical under all three rotations: Laya-multilingual 53%, Julia-1 35%, laya-bangla 67%) and is small for Jev (93%) and d3 (87-89%). | Fig. 6 | Three rotations only |
| C6 | The harness reproduces independent numbers: Jev Belebele 0.918 vs published 0.917; Laya-multilingual MASSIVE 0.179 vs 0.18 and SIB-200 0.588 vs 0.57-0.58 (laya-bangla authors); laya-bangla SIB-200 0.750 vs 0.76, SentNoB 0.482 vs 0.48-0.49. Laya's SentNoB score is wording-sensitive (macro-F1 0.263 to 0.376). | analysis/RESULTS_AUDIT.md | Only some tasks comparable |

Not claimed: that this is the first Bengali System One study (a gap table in Related Work states what each prior paper covers); any effect of fine-tuning; any statement about Jev's training data; any result for Kev, d3 (27B), or Microsoft-Decision-1.


Update 2026-10-23: Kev-0.8B and Kev-4B added (test sets and AI-generated Banglish items only; no dev split or rotations). Kev-4B is level with d3-lite; both Kev models are far below Jev. Banglish - rule-based: +10.7 (Kev-4B), +11.4 (Kev-0.8B), inside the range of the other models. Rank agreement over 11 systems: tau 0.89 (rule-based), 0.93 (natural-style). Fixed: the SentNoB majority-class baseline is a macro-F1 of 0.195, not 0.412 (that is an accuracy).
